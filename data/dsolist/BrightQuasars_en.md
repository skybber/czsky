Bright quasars

Selection of quasars and BL Lac objects within reach of amateur telescopes

Quasars are extremely luminous nuclei of distant galaxies powered by matter falling into a supermassive black hole.
In a telescope they look like ordinary stars, but their light has travelled for billions of years. The listed distance
is the light-travel distance (lookback time) computed from the redshift for Planck 2018 cosmology.

The selection contains:

1. quasars and BL Lac objects brighter than 15 mag with redshift z ≥ 0.15
1. distant quasars with redshift z ≥ 2.5 brighter than 16.5 mag
1. well-known objects such as 3C 273, Ton 618, Twin Quasar, Einstein Cross, OJ 287 or BL Lacertae

Data are taken from [Véron-Cetty & Véron, Catalogue of Quasars and Active Nuclei, 13th edition](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/cat/VII/258).
Many quasars and especially BL Lac objects are variable, their actual brightness may differ from the catalogue value
by several magnitudes.
